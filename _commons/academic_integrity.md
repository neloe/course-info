_Updated Fall 2024_

This document presents the position of faculty teaching courses in the areas of computer science and/or information systems at Northwest Missouri State University. 
This document is consistent with the University's code of academic integrity. 

The following represents a non-exhaustive list of examples of activities violating the eight areas of academic behavior comprising  the Code of Academic Integrity: 

1.	knowingly giving or receiving unauthorized aid or information
    *	Allowing another student to have access to or sharing your login credentials
    *	Providing solutions, access codes, or information about an assignment or assessment
    *	Sharing files
1.	committing plagiarism
    * Failing to properly attribute external code or assistance received
    * Obvious copying of code, same solutions with different words
1.	misrepresenting facts or data
    * Falsely claiming a class or event was attended if attendance is used as a metric for evaluation
    * Providing incorrect or false citation information
    * Citing references that do not exist 
    * Modifying file properties 
    * Extrapolating conclusions based on non-referenced dataset (or previous analyses of other datasets).
1.	offering bribes
    * Persuading faculty, staff, teaching assistants, graduate assistants, or students to act in one’s favor by providing money, acts, or gifts to change a grade or complete homework assignment
1.	using library, computer, or other university resources unethically
    * Using a school laptop to illegally distribute files or data.
    * Using others’ computer to submit assignments while not logged in under your own credentials 
1.	knowingly engaging in acts of deception, falsification, or misrepresentation that interfere with equitable academic assessment and evaluation
    * Swiping someone else’s Bearcat card for attendance. 
    * Submitting an assignment or assessment for someone else 
    * Stealing someone’s laptop 
    * Modifying a hard copy of an exam or assignment after returned 
    * Exiting the exam room or bypassing Lockdown Browser without submitting the assessment
1.	publishing or transmitting academic materials authored by an instructor, including study guides, exams, and assignments, without the author’s permission
    * Distributing screenshots or pictures of assessments
    * Posting instructor solutions online
    * Sharing assignments or labs from a previous semester
1.	knowingly assisting in any of the above practices
